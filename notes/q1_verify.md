## 2. Detailed comments

### Setup
* Witness ⇒ curve. ed^[2] is a minimum over all F' with [F':K] ≤ 2. A purely inseparable quadratic F' has ed_k(τ_gen ⊗ F') = ed_k(τ_gen) ≥ 2 (Prop 3.6, Thm 7.1), so only separable K' = K(v_f) matter; the proposal should say so. For K' ≠ K(z0), L' = L ⊗_K K' is a field (K(z0) is the unique quadratic subfield of L/K), Cor 4.4 gives a smooth projective C with faithful G-action and a dominant equivariant Spec L' → C, and K' = K(z0) is excluded by Prop 3.9. CORRECT.
* Descent point. The quotient of Spec L' → C by G is Spec K' → B := C/G (Lemma 2.3's proof); its image is the generic point of B, so k(B) ↪ K' and the point is non-constant. C → B is a Z/8-torsor over the free locus, with class β ∈ W_3(k(B))/℘ pulling back to s ⊗ K' (Lemma 2.3: f^*(π) ≅ τ), so k(B) is a "descent field" in the sense of Thm 11.6. CORRECT.
* Q ∈ E_2(K'). The quotient of C by the normal subgroup <g^4> carries a faithful action of Z/4 = G/<g^4> (if h acts trivially on C/<g^4> then h g^{-4i} acts trivially on C for some i, by irreducibility). The <g^4>-quotient of the equivariant map is an equivariant Spec(L'^{<g^4>}) → C_2. Here L'^{<g^4>} = L^{<g^4>} ⊗_K K' = k(z0,z1,s2) ⊗_K K' = k(z0,z1,s2,v_f): a field (a K'-subalgebra of the field L', finite over K'), and it is the base change to K' of the Z/4-torsor Spec k(z0,z1,s2) → Spec K of class (s0,s1) (truncation of ℘(z) = s). By Fact 2.2(a) (twist commutes with base change) this is a K'-point Q of E_2 := τ_{(s0,s1)_K} C_2. Its image in B_K is the K'-point of B obtained above (C → C_2 → B is the G-quotient), hence non-constant. CORRECT.
* Hidden assumptions: none beyond k algebraically closed (used throughout the paper), char 2, and K' ≠ K(z0). Q need not be free or affine.

### Step 1
* Pic of the twist. E_2 ⊗_K L_2' ≅ C_2 ⊗_k L_2' (T ×_K T ≅ G × T), and E_2 is the Galois descent of C_2 ⊗ L_2' along L_2'/K with descent datum twisted by the G-action. Pic_{X/F} commutes with base field extension for smooth projective curves and is functorial, so Pic_{E_2/K} is the descent of Pic_{C_2/k} ⊗ L_2' with the twisted datum, i.e. τ(Pic_{C_2/k}) with the induced action (degree preserved). Pic^2_{C_2/k} is a projective variety (Jac-torsor), so Fact 2.2(a) applies: Pic^2_{E_2/K}(K) = Hom_G(Spec L_2', Pic^2_{C_2/k}). CORRECT (standard descent; worth one sentence of justification in a write-up).
* Constancy. A k-morphism Spec L_2' → Pic^2_{C_2} is a rational map A^3 ⇢ (torsor under an abelian variety), which extends to a morphism (Weil's extension theorem for maps from smooth varieties to abelian torsors) and is constant (A^3 is covered by lines and P^1 → abelian variety is constant, Lemma 4.1(ii)'s argument). Equivariance forces the value to be G-fixed. CORRECT.
* Passage to divisors. The K-point [Q+σQ] of Pic_{E_2/K}, viewed as an L_2'-point of Pic_{C_2 ⊗ L_2'/L_2'} via E_2 ⊗ L_2' ≅ C_2 ⊗ L_2', is the constant class [D] with D a degree-2 divisor on C_2 over k. Since C_2(k) ≠ ∅, Pic(C_2 ⊗ L_2') → Pic_{C_2/k}(L_2') is an isomorphism (no Brauer obstruction), so (Q+σQ)_{L_2'} ∼ D_{L_2'} on C_2 ⊗ L_2'. CORRECT.
* Minor gap (FIXABLE): the proposal writes ℓ(D) ∈ {1,2} but a general degree-2 class on a curve of genus ≥ 3 has ℓ = 0. Here ℓ_k(D) = ℓ_{L_2'}(D_{L_2'}) ≥ 1 because the class contains the effective divisor (Q+σQ)_{L_2'} (h^0 commutes with flat base change). So one may and must choose D effective over k; then D = P_1 + P_2 with P_i ∈ C_2(k). With this, Step 2a's "D is constant (supported on k-points)" is justified.

### Step 2
* Bounds. deg D = 2, g ≥ 1: Riemann–Roch gives ℓ(D) = 2 for g = 1; for g ≥ 2, D is special (deg < 2g−2, or = 2g−2 for g = 2 where D ∼ K_C is the g^1_2) and Clifford gives ℓ(D) ≤ 2 with equality iff C_2 is hyperelliptic and D is the g^1_2 (for g = 2 every curve is hyperelliptic and the canonical class is the g^1_2). In particular ℓ(D) ≤ 1 for every degree-2 divisor on a non-hyperelliptic curve of genus ≥ 2. CORRECT.
* (2a) ℓ_k(D) = 1 ⇒ ℓ_{L_2'}(D) = 1 (flat base change) ⇒ D_{L_2'} = P_1 + P_2 is the unique effective member of its class, so (Q+σQ)_{L_2'} = P_1 + P_2 as divisors on C_2 ⊗ L_2'. Let x be the closed point of E_2 which is the image of Q (degree 1 or 2 over K); x ⊗_K L_2' is supported on {P_1, P_2} × Spec L_2'. The L''-point of C_2 ⊗ L_2' induced by Q (L'' = K' ⊗_K L_2', a field) lies on x ⊗ L_2', so the composite Spec L'' → C_2 — which is precisely the equivariant map corresponding to Q — factors through some P_i ∈ C_2(k). Then Spec K' → B factors through the image of P_i, contradicting non-constancy. CORRECT. (The case Q ∈ E_2(K), Q+σQ = 2Q, is included.)
* (2b) as stated. CORRECT.

### Step 3 (hyperelliptic C_2, g ≥ 2)
* (i) The g^1_2 is unique for g ≥ 2 in every characteristic (two distinct g^1_2 give a birational map to a (2,2)-curve in P^1 × P^1, of genus ≤ 1), hence Aut(C_2)-stable; Z/4 acts on C_2/ι = P^1_u. CORRECT.
* (ii) The kernel of Aut(C_2) → Aut(P^1_u) is {1, ι} (an automorphism preserving every fibre of the degree-2 map is 1 or ι). Lemma 2.5(ii) (p = 2: no element of order 4 in PGL_2(k)) forces the image of Z/4 to have order ≤ 2, so the kernel has order ≥ 2, hence equals {1,ι} = <g^2>. CORRECT.
* (iii) An element of order 2 in PGL_2(k), char 2, lifts to A = 1 + N, N ≠ 0 nilpotent (proof of Lemma 2.5(ii)), conjugate to a Jordan block, i.e. u ↦ u + 1. CORRECT.
* (iv) B = P^1_u/(u ↦ u+1) = P^1_t, t = u^2 + u (k(u)^{Z/2} = k(u^2+u)). CORRECT.
* (v) g^2 acts trivially on P^1_u, so τ(P^1_u) = (T/<g^2> × P^1_u)/(Z/2) with T/<g^2> = Spec k(z0,s1,s2), the Z/2-torsor z0^2 + z0 = s0, i.e. the truncation of (s0,s1) to W_1. The function u + z0 is invariant under the diagonal action (both shifted by 1; no sign issue in char 2), so ũ := u + z0 is a K-rational coordinate on τP^1_u ≅ P^1_K, exactly as h = y − z in Prop 5.2(a). The map E_2 → P^1_K is the twist of the degree-2 map C_2 → P^1_u. CORRECT.
* (vi) Gap to be stated, FIXABLE: Step 1 gives (Q+σQ)_{L_2'} ∼ D_{L_2'} = (fibre of u)_{L_2'} = (F_c)_{L_2'} for a fibre F_c of ũ over c ∈ P^1(K) (over L_2', ũ-fibres are u-fibres). To conclude Q + σQ ∼ F_c over K one uses that Pic(E_2) → Pic_{E_2/K}(K) is injective (Leray: 0 → Pic X → Pic_{X/K}(K) → Br K) and Pic_{E_2/K}(K) → Pic_{E_2/K}(L_2') is injective (points of a scheme). Then L(F_c) over K is 2-dimensional (base change) and contains 1 and 1/(ũ − c), so the effective K-rational members of |F_c| are exactly the ũ-fibres over P^1(K). Hence Q + σQ = ũ^{-1}(c') with c' ∈ P^1(K), and ũ(Q) = c' ∈ K ∪ {∞}. (c' = ∞ gives t(Q) = ∞, a constant descent point, contradiction anyway.) CORRECT with this addition.
* (vii) t is Z/4-invariant on C_2, so it descends unchanged to E_2 → B_K; t = u^2 + u = (ũ + z0)^2 + (ũ + z0) = ũ^2 + ũ + s0. So t(Q) = c'^2 + c' + s0 ∈ K, and the descent embedding k(B) = k(t) → K', t ↦ t(Q), lands in K: F_0 ⊂ K, i.e. Case A, contradicting Thm 11.6(b) (whose proof I re-checked: truncation of s ≡ β + ε(0,0,f) to W_2 gives ed_k((s0,s1)_K) ≤ 1 against Lemma 4.1(i) + Prop 6.1). CORRECT.

### Step 4(i) (C_2 = E_0 with the Witt action)
* By ed2_analysis Lemma 1.3 (re-checked: t_Q φ t_{−Q} = t_{(1−α)Q} α with 1 − α surjective on E(k)), a faithful Z/4 on the supersingular curve is conjugate to one fixing O, and is (E_0, Witt action) up to inverting the generator; the fixed point ∞ is unique (Thm 6.2).
* Jac(k)^G = 0: the proposal's phrase "as 2-rank 0" is incomplete; what is needed is that g acts on Jac(E_0) = E_0 as an automorphism α of order 4 with α^2 = −1, so deg(1 − α) = 2 and ker(1−α)(k) ⊂ E_0[2](k) = 0 (supersingular, char 2). This is the argument of ed4 Lemma 5.1. FIXABLE (one line). Then [D − 2∞] ∈ Pic^0(E_0)(k)^G = 0, so D ∼ 2∞.
* |2∞'|_K = {u-fibres}: as in Step 3(vi), Q + σQ ∼ 2∞' over K (Pic(E_2) ↪ Pic_{E_2/K}(K)), L(2∞') = K·1 + K·u with u = y0 + z0 K-rational (Thm 6.2, u has a double pole at ∞'), so Q + σQ = u^{-1}(c), u(Q) = c ∈ K (c = ∞ means Q = ∞', constant). Inverting the generator replaces (s0,s1) by −(s0,s1) = (s0, s1 + s0^2) and changes nothing (u = y0 + z0 is still invariant since g^{-1}: y0 ↦ y0 + 1 as −1 = (1,1) in W_2(F_2)).
* t = y0^2 + y0 = u^2 + u + s0 is the coordinate of B = E_0/(Z/4) = P^1_t (Thm 6.2), so t(Q) ∈ K and k(B) ⊂ K: Case A, contradiction with Thm 11.6(b). CORRECT. Note: Cor 11.7 is stated for U_3 only; here C is an arbitrary Z/8-curve with C/<g^4> = E_0, and the proposal's argument is the correct generalisation (it does not need Prop 7.5).

### Step 4(ii) (ordinary E, translation by a point P of order 4)
* Structure: an ordinary elliptic curve in char 2 has j ≠ 0, Aut(E,O) = {±1}; φ = t_P ∘ [−1] has φ^2 = t_{P − P} = 1, so an element of order 4 is a translation t_P with ord P = 4 (E[4](k) ≅ Z/4 exists). B = E/<P> is elliptic and C_2 → B is the étale isogeny with kernel <P> ≅ Z/4. CORRECT.
* (s0,s1)_K ⊗ K' = δ_{K'}(Q_B): Q corresponds to an equivariant T_{K'} → C_2 lying over Q_B, hence to an equivariant map of Z/4-torsors T_{K'} → φ^{-1}(Q_B) over K', which is an isomorphism; φ^{-1}(Q_B) with the translation action of ker φ ≅ Z/4 (1 ↦ P, matching g ↦ t_P) is by definition the torsor δ_{K'}(Q_B). CORRECT (a different identification Z/4 ≅ ker φ would change the class by an automorphism of Z/4, irrelevant for "2x ≠ 0").
* cores ∘ δ_{K'} = δ_K ∘ N_{K'/K}: standard. Apply Weil restriction π_* (exact for finite étale K'/K) to 0 → Z/4 → C_2 → B → 0; H^1(K, π_*Z/4) = H^1(K', Z/4) (Shapiro), (π_*B)(K) = B(K'), the connecting map is δ_{K'}; the norm/trace maps π_*π^*(−) → (−) form a morphism of short exact sequences, and cores is by definition induced by the trace on Z/4. CORRECT.
* cores ∘ res = [K':K] = 2 on H^1(K, Z/4). CORRECT. Hence δ_K(Q_B + σQ_B) = 2·(s0,s1).
* Witt identity: 2x = VFx for p = 2, so 2(s0,s1) = (0, s0^2) (checked by ghost components: 2(a0,a1) = (0,a0^2)); (0,s0^2) − (0,s0) = V(℘s0) = ℘V(s0) ∈ ℘W_2(K); and (0,s0) ∉ ℘W_2(K) by (5) since s0 ∉ ℘K. So 2(s0,s1) ≠ 0 in H^1(K, Z/4). CORRECT.
* B(K) = B(k): Lemma 4.1(ii) with F = k, F' = K = k(s0,s1,s2). Q_B + σQ_B is σ-invariant hence in B(K) (B is defined over k). CORRECT.
* δ_K(b) = 0 for b ∈ B(k): φ^{-1}(b) is a Z/4-torsor over k, split since k is algebraically closed; its base change to K is split. CORRECT.
* Conclusion 2(s0,s1) = 0, contradiction. CORRECT. Remark: this step does not use Step 1, nor the non-constancy of Q_B; it shows that res_{K'/K}(s0,s1) is never an isogeny class δ_{K'}(Q_B) for any Q_B ∈ B(K'), any quadratic K'/K.

### Completeness of the case split
* g(C_2) = 0: Z/4 ↪ PGL_2(k) impossible (Lemma 2.5(ii)). g(C_2) = 1: the supersingular curve is unique and Lemma 1.3 applies; the ordinary case is Step 4(ii) (no other elements of order 4, see above). g(C_2) ≥ 2: Step 1 gives an effective D; ℓ(D) = 1 is Step 2a (covers all non-hyperelliptic curves and hyperelliptic curves with D ≁ g^1_2), ℓ(D) = 2 is Step 3. Every faithful Z/4-curve C_2 = C/<g^4> falls in exactly one case. CORRECT.
* Hidden assumptions checked: k algebraically closed (used for Pic(C_2 ⊗ L_2') = Pic_{C_2/k}(L_2'), for B(k)-points, for splitting of constant torsors); K' ≠ K(z0) (L' a field); separability (Prop 3.6); Q + σQ interpreted as the K-rational degree-2 divisor associated with the closed point of Q (works also when Q ∈ E_2(K)); C_2 → B is the quotient by Z/4 = G/<g^4> because C → B is the quotient by G. No further assumptions are needed.

### By-product: ed4 Proposition 7.1
* Prop 7.1 asserts δ_K(Q + σQ) = 2s ≠ 0 with Q + σQ ∈ C'(K). But ed4's own input (I5) (Lemma 4.1(ii)) gives C'(K) = C'(k), and δ_K vanishes on C'(k) (constant fibres are split k-torsors). So Prop 7.1's hypothesis (the isogeny shape s = δ_{K'}(Q), Q ∈ C'(K')) is contradictory: the isogeny shape is impossible outright. The proposal's claim is CORRECT. In particular the statements "Q + σQ has exact order 8 in C'(K)/φ(C(K))" and the conclusion that this is merely a "necessary condition" are wrong in ed4: C'(K)/φ(C(K)) = C'(k)/φ(C(k)) = 0. The same applies at level 2 (2(s0,s1) ≠ 0), which is Step 4(ii).
* The proposal also says "a witness whose cover C → B is étale" is impossible. For g(B) = 1 this is the isogeny shape. For g(B) ≥ 2 one needs one more step: a cyclic étale cover C → B of degree 8 is the pullback of a Z/8-isogeny A → Jac(B) along an Abel–Jacobi map (π_1^ab(B) = π_1(Jac B)), so s ⊗ K' = δ_{K'}(AJ(Q_B)) and the norm argument runs with Jac(B)(K) = Jac(B)(k) (Lemma 4.1(ii) for abelian varieties). FIXABLE, and in any case not needed for the main claim: the main argument covers every C_2 regardless of ramification (an étale C → B with g(B) ≥ 2 has g(C_2) ≥ 2 and falls under Steps 2–3).

## 3. Overall verdict

**The argument proves ed^[2]_k(τ_gen) = 2 at (p,n) = (2,3), subject to the small, routine additions listed below. I found no wrong step and no unjustified identification that resists repair.** Every case of the split is closed by one of three independent mechanisms, each of which I checked against the cited sources:

1. Constancy of K-rational degree-2 classes on the twist E_2 (Step 1: twist/Picard descent, rigidity of A^3 → abelian torsor), giving either a constant point (Step 2a) or a K-rational member of a twisted g^1_2 / |2∞'| (Steps 3, 4(i)), which forces the descent field into K and hits Theorem 11.6(b).
2. Theorem 11.6(b) itself (Case A empty), whose proof I re-verified.
3. The corestriction/norm argument in the isogeny case (Step 4(ii)), which uses only Lemma 4.1(ii), cores∘res = 2, the Witt identity 2(s0,s1) = (0,s0^2) ≡ (0,s0) ≠ 0 (checked computationally), and the vanishing of δ_K on constant points.

Items that a written version must add (all FIXABLE, none affecting the conclusion):
* (a) Purely inseparable quadratic extensions: cite Prop 3.6 (ed unchanged, hence ≥ 2).
* (b) Step 1/2: the class of D contains an effective divisor because ℓ(D_{L_2'}) ≥ 1 and ℓ is invariant under base change; take D = P_1 + P_2 with P_i ∈ C_2(k). Without this, "ℓ(D) ∈ {1,2}" and "D supported on k-points" are unjustified.
* (c) Steps 3(vi), 4(i): the linear equivalence Q + σQ ∼ (fibre) obtained over L_2' descends to K because Pic(E_2) ↪ Pic_{E_2/K}(K) ↪ Pic_{E_2/K}(L_2'); then |F_c|_K = {ũ-fibres over P^1(K)} since L(F_c) = <1, 1/(ũ−c)>.
* (d) Step 4(i): Jac(E_0)(k)^G = 0 because deg(1 − α) = 2 and E_0[2](k) = 0 ("2-rank 0" alone is not enough); and Cor 11.7 should be cited as the special case C = U_3, not as the proof.
* (e) Step 4(ii): state the compatibility cores_{K'/K} ∘ δ_{K'} = δ_K ∘ N_{K'/K} (Weil restriction of the isogeny sequence + Shapiro) and note that the identification Z/4 ≅ ker φ only matters up to Aut(Z/4).
* (f) Step 1: one sentence that Pic_{E_2/K} = τ(Pic_{C_2/k}) by Galois descent along L_2'/K (Pic commutes with base change; descent datum is functorial).

Consequences if the above is accepted: d_1(τ_gen) ∈ {3,4} (d_1 ≥ 3 since ed^[2] = 2 and ed^[3] ∈ {1,2}, and d_1 ≤ 4 by Thm 7.1); ed4's Prop 7.1 and its "remaining case (2)" are superseded (the isogeny shape is contradictory), and ed4's remaining cases (1), (3) are also closed by this argument. The open question whether ed^[3] = 1 (cubic witnesses) is untouched: Step 4(ii) and Step 1 use the norm over a quadratic extension and σ-invariance of Q + σQ in an essential way.

Points where I was most sceptical and why I ended up accepting them: (i) whether the twist of Pic is Pic of the twist — yes, by descent along the Galois extension L_2'/K over which the twist trivialises; (ii) whether "Q + σQ ∼ D over L_2'" gives information over K — yes, via injectivity of Pic(E_2) into the Picard scheme; (iii) whether Step 4(ii) is "too strong" — it says res_{K'/K}(s0,s1) is never an isogeny class over any quadratic K', which is consistent with the level-1 analogue (where 2·s0 = 0 gives no obstruction) and with ed4's own Prop 7.1 once (I5) is applied; I found no counterexample.
