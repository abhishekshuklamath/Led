# The finite-degree profile of essential dimension: general propositions (characteristic 0 / characteristic prime to |G|)

Companion note to *The finite-degree profile of essential dimension of a Z/p^n-torsor in characteristic p* (file `ed_profile_paper.txt`, cited below as **[P]**). Everything here concerns a *finite* group $G$ and a base field $k$; the standing hypothesis for the cohomological arguments is $\operatorname{char} k = 0$ (or $\operatorname{char} k \nmid |G|$, $k$ perfect), but several statements are valid in every characteristic and are marked so.

All citations are **from memory** (no literature access while writing); each is flagged `[cited: …]` and should be checked against the source before use.

## 1. Conventions

$k$ is a field, $G$ a finite group (viewed as a constant group scheme over $k$), $F\supseteq k$ a field, $\tau\colon T\to\operatorname{Spec}F$ a $G$-torsor. The **classifying homomorphism** $\varphi_\tau\colon\Gamma_F=\operatorname{Gal}(F^{\rm sep}/F)\to G$ is well defined up to conjugation; its image $H=\operatorname{im}\varphi_\tau$ is well defined up to conjugation, and $T\cong\coprod_{G/H}\operatorname{Spec}E$ with $E/F$ Galois with group $H$ (the **splitting field** of $\tau$; $\tau\otimes F'$ is split iff $F'$ contains an $F$-copy of $E$).

$\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)$ is the least $\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0$ over subfields $k\subseteq F_0\subseteq F$ with $\tau\cong\tau_0\otimes_{F_0}F$. Basic facts used throughout, valid in every characteristic:

* (B1) $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes_FF')\le\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)$ for every field extension $F'/F$ (pull back the descent).
* (B2) $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)\ge\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(\tau)$ for $k\subseteq k'\subseteq F$ (a descent $F_0$ over $k$ gives the descent $F_0k'$ over $k'$, and $\operatorname{trdeg}_{k'}F_0k'\le\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0$ since a transcendence basis of $F_0/k$ generates $F_0k'$ over $k'$ up to algebraic elements).
* (B3) $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)=0$ iff $\tau$ descends to the algebraic closure $k_F$ of $k$ in $F$. If $k$ is algebraically closed this says: $\tau$ is split. For general $k$ it does **not** say "$\tau$ descends to $k$" (example: $k=\mathbb Q$, $F=\mathbb Q(i)(t)$, $\tau=F(\sqrt{1+i})$: $\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q}=0$ but $\tau\not\cong F(\sqrt a)$ for $a\in\mathbb Q$ because $N_{\mathbb Q(i)/\mathbb Q}(1+i)=2$ is not a rational square).
* (B4) [P, Lemma 4.1(i)] If $F$ is infinite and $t$ transcendental over $F$, then $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F(t))=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)$ (any characteristic; the proof in [P] uses only spreading out and an $F$-point of a dense open of $\mathbb A^1_F$).

$\mathrm{ed}_k(G)=\sup_\tau\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)$. For a prime $q$: $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)=\min\{\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F'):F'/F\text{ finite separable},\ q\nmid[F':F]\}$ and $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)=\sup_\tau\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)$ [cited: Reichstein–Scavia (2.5); Merkurjev, *Essential dimension: a survey*].

**Generic torsor.** Fix a faithful representation $V$ of $G$ over $k$ (or any faithful generically free $G$-variety with $k(V)$ regular over $k$). $L=k(V)$, $K=L^G$, and $\tau_{\rm gen}\colon\operatorname{Spec}L\to\operatorname{Spec}K$. Then $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})=\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$ [cited: Berhuy–Favi; Merkurjev; also [P, Lemma 2.1]]. The **profile** is Definition 3.1 of [P]:
$$\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)=\min\{\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes_FF'):[F':F]\le d\},\qquad d_j(\tau)=\min\{d\ge1:\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)\le j\}.$$

## 2. P1: shape of the profile (any characteristic, any $k$)

**Proposition 2.1.** Let $\tau$ be a $G$-torsor over $F\supseteq k$.

(a) $d\mapsto\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)$ is non-increasing with values in $\{0,\dots,\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)\}$, $\mathrm{ed}^{[1]}_k(\tau)=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)$, and $d_{\mathrm{ed}(\tau)}(\tau)=1\le d_{\mathrm{ed}(\tau)-1}(\tau)\le\dots\le d_0(\tau)\le|\operatorname{im}\varphi_\tau|<\infty$.

(b) (composition) If $[F':F]=e$ and $d\ge1$ then $\mathrm{ed}^{[de]}_k(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau\otimes F')\le\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)$. Consequently $d_j(\tau)\le[F':F]\cdot d_j(\tau\otimes F')$ for every finite $F'/F$ and every $j$; in particular, if $F_i$ is a witness for $d_i(\tau)$ (i.e. $[F_i:F]=d_i(\tau)$ and $\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F_i)\le i$) then $d_j(\tau)\le d_i(\tau)\,d_j(\tau\otimes F_i)$ for all $j\le i$.

(c) (jump degrees determine the profile) $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)=\min\{j:d_j(\tau)\le d\}$; thus the profile is the step function with steps at $d_{\mathrm{ed}(\tau)-1},\dots,d_0$.

(d) (base field) If $k\subseteq k'\subseteq F$ then $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_{k'}(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)$ for all $d$, hence $d_j^{k'}(\tau)\le d_j^{k}(\tau)$. If $k'\not\subseteq F$, the comparison is with $\tau\otimes F'$ for a composite $F'$ of $F$ and $k'$; see P3.

(e) $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)=0$ iff there is $F'/F$ of degree $\le d$ such that $\tau\otimes F'$ descends to the algebraic closure of $k$ in $F'$. If $k$ is algebraically closed: iff $\tau$ is split by an extension of degree $\le d$.

*Proof.* (a) Monotonicity and the values are clear from the definition and (B1); the bound $d_0\le|H|$, $H=\operatorname{im}\varphi_\tau$: over the splitting field $E$ ($[E:F]=|H|$) the torsor is split, hence descends to $k$. (b) is [P, Prop. 3.2(b)] verbatim (an extension $F''/F'$ of degree $\le d$ has $[F'':F]\le de$; conversely for $[F_1:F]\le d$ any composite field $F_1F'$ has $[F_1F':F']\le d$ and $\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F_1F')\le\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F_1)$ by (B1)). For the consequence: if $[F'':F']= d_j(\tau\otimes F')$ realises $\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F'')\le j$, then $[F'':F]=[F':F]d_j(\tau\otimes F')$. (c) is a restatement. (d) follows from (B2) applied to each $\tau\otimes F'$ (note $k'\subseteq F\subseteq F'$). (e) is (B3). $\square$

**Remark 2.2.** (i) The profile of $\tau$ is *not* an invariant of the class of $\tau$ in $H^1(F,G)$ alone but of the pair $(\tau,k)$; by (d) enlarging $k$ inside $F$ can only lower it. (ii) Nothing in P1 uses the characteristic, and (a)–(c),(e) hold for any (not necessarily constant) finite étale group scheme $G$ once "$|\operatorname{im}\varphi_\tau|$" is replaced by the degree of a splitting field. (iii) In [P, Prop. 3.6] the profile in characteristic $p$ is shown to see only separable extensions (for $G$ a $p$-group); Lemma 4.2 below gives the analogous statement for $\operatorname{char}k\nmid|G|$, $k$ perfect.

## 3. P2: the last jump degree $d_0$

Let $H=\operatorname{im}\varphi_\tau$ and $E$ the splitting field of $\tau$, $[E:F]=|H|$.

**Proposition 3.1.** (a) (any $k$, any characteristic) $d_0(\tau)\le d_{\rm split}(\tau):=\min\{[F':F]:\tau\otimes F'\text{ split}\}=|H|$.

(b) If $k$ is algebraically closed then $d_0(\tau)=|H|$.

(c) Suppose $k$ is perfect and $E$ is a regular extension of $k$ (i.e. $k$ is algebraically closed in $E$; separability is automatic for $k$ perfect). Then $d_0(\tau)=|H|$; more precisely, if $[F':F]<\infty$ and $\tau\otimes F'$ descends to the algebraic closure $k_1$ of $k$ in $F'$, then $[F':F]\ge|H|\cdot[k_1:k]$.

(d) For $\tau_{\rm gen}$ (any faithful $V$ with $k(V)/k$ regular, $k$ perfect): $d_0(\tau_{\rm gen})=|G|$, and every $F'/K$ with $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')=0$ satisfies $[F':K]\ge|G|[k_1:k]$.

*Proof.* (a) $E\otimes_FF'\cong\prod_i E F'_i$ (composita inside an algebraic closure of $F'$), so $T\otimes_FF'$ is split, i.e. $\cong\coprod_G\operatorname{Spec}F'$, iff every composite $EF'_i$ equals $F'$, iff $E$ embeds $F$-linearly into $F'$; this forces $[F':F]\ge|H|$ and $F'=E$ works. A split torsor has $\mathrm{ed}=0$, so $d_0\le|H|$. (b) By (B3), $\mathrm{ed}=0$ iff split, so $d_0=d_{\rm split}$.

(c) Let $\tau\otimes F'\cong\tau_0\otimes_{k_1}F'$ with $\tau_0$ a $G$-torsor over $k_1$; its total space is $\coprod_{G/H_0}\operatorname{Spec}l$ with $l/k_1$ Galois with group $H_0$, so $[l:k_1]=|H_0|$. Comparing total spaces over $F'$, the field factors of $E\otimes_FF'$ and of $l\otimes_{k_1}F'$ coincide; fix embeddings into an algebraic closure $\Omega$ of $F'$ so that $EF'=lF'$ as subfields of $\Omega$. Now $l/k$ is finite separable and $E/k$ regular, so $l\otimes_kE$ is a field and $[lE:E]=[l:k]=|H_0|[k_1:k]$. Since $lE\subseteq lF'=EF'$,
$$|H_0|[k_1:k]\cdot|H|=[lE:E][E:F]\le[EF':F]=[lF':F']\,[F':F]\le[l:k_1]\,[F':F]=|H_0|\,[F':F],$$
whence $[F':F]\ge|H|[k_1:k]$. Since $F'=E$ gives $\mathrm{ed}=0$, $d_0(\tau)=|H|$.

(d) $E=L=k(V)$ is regular over $k$ and $H=G$ ($L/K$ is Galois with group $G$). $\square$

**Remark 3.2.** (i) Without regularity, $d_0<|H|$ is possible: $k=\mathbb Q$, $F=\mathbb Q(t)$, $\tau=F(\sqrt2)/F$ has $|H|=2$ but $d_0=1$ ($\tau$ is constant). (ii) (c) shows that for $\tau_{\rm gen}$ "$\mathrm{ed}=0$ at degree $|G|$" is only reachable by extensions in which $k$ stays algebraically closed, e.g. by $F'=L$. (iii) This is the general form of [P, Prop. 3.2(c)] ($d_0(\tau_{\rm gen})=p^n$), whose proof is the $k$ algebraically closed case. (iv) For every $G$ and every $\tau$, the profile therefore ends exactly at $d=|H|$ when $k$ is algebraically closed: $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}(\tau)\ge1$ for $d<|H|$. This is the only slot of the profile that is completely understood in general.

## 4. P3: the base-field extension bound (any characteristic)

**Proposition 4.1.** Let $k'/k$ be a finite extension of degree $d$ and $\tau$ a $G$-torsor over $F\supseteq k$. Then
$$\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(G).$$
More precisely, for every field factor $F'$ of $F\otimes_kk'$ one has $[F':F]\le d$ and $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')\le\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(\tau\otimes F')\le\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(G)$.

*Proof.* $F'$ is a quotient of $F\otimes_kk'$, so $[F':F]\le d$, and $F'\supseteq k'$. Let $\tau\otimes F'\cong\tau_0\otimes_{F_0}F'$ with $k'\subseteq F_0\subseteq F'$ and $\operatorname{trdeg}_{k'}F_0=\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(\tau\otimes F')\le\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(G)$. Since $k'/k$ is algebraic, $\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0=\operatorname{trdeg}_{k'}F_0$, and $F_0\supseteq k$, so the same descent shows $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')\le\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (roots-of-unity defect).** Let $e$ be the exponent of $G$, $\operatorname{char}k\nmid e$, and $k_e=k(\mu_e)$, $d_e=[k_e:k]$ (a divisor of $\varphi(e)$). Then for every $G$-torsor $\tau$ over every $F\supseteq k$,
$$\mathrm{ed}^{[d_e]}_k(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G),\qquad\text{in fact }\mathrm{ed}^{[[F(\mu_e):F]]}_k(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G).$$
In particular:

1. If $G$ is abelian, $\mathrm{ed}^{[d_e]}_k(\tau)\le\operatorname{rank}G$ (the number of cyclic factors) [cited: Buhler–Reichstein, $\mathrm{ed}_{k}(G)=\operatorname{rank}G$ for abelian $G$ when $\mu_e\subset k$; upper bound by the diagonal representation, lower bound by their Theorem 6.1 / by Karpenko–Merkurjev].
2. If $G$ is a $p$-group and $\operatorname{char}k\ne p$, then $\mathrm{ed}^{[p-1]}_k(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}^{[[k(\mu_p):k]]}_k(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_p)}(G)=\min\{\dim W: W\text{ a faithful }k(\mu_p)\text{-representation of }G\}$ [cited: Karpenko–Merkurjev, *Essential dimension of finite p-groups*, Invent. Math. 2008, valid over any field containing $\mu_p$ of characteristic $\ne p$].
3. Combining with (B2): $\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G)\le\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$ and the profile of $\tau_{\rm gen}$ drops from $\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$ to at most $\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G)$ within degree $d_e$; the profile therefore "sees" the difference $\mathrm{ed}_k(G)-\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G)$, which is invisible to $\mathrm{ed}(\,\cdot\,;q)$ for $q\nmid d_e$ (Lemma 5.3 below).

*Proof.* The first inequality is Prop. 4.1 with $k'=k(\mu_e)$; for the refinement note that a field factor of $F\otimes_kk(\mu_e)$ is $F(\mu_e)$, of degree $[F(\mu_e):F]\le d_e$. The three items are the quoted theorems. $\square$

**Remark 4.3.** (i) The bound is sharp in the trivial sense that for $\tau=\tau_{\rm gen}$ over $k$ and $F'=K(\mu_e)$ the torsor $\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F'$ is the generic torsor over $k(\mu_e)$ (if $\mu_e\not\subset K$, i.e. if $k$ is algebraically closed in $K$, which holds for $K=k(V)^G$), so $\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes K(\mu_e))=\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G)$ exactly, and $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes K(\mu_e))\in[\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G),\ \min(\mathrm{ed}_k(G),\ \mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G)+\ldots)]$; I do not know a general reason for $\mathrm{ed}_k=\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}$ on this particular torsor, so the value of $\mathrm{ed}^{[d_e]}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})$ is in general only bounded above by Cor. 4.2. [open in general] (ii) Example E3 (§8) shows that the bound can be strictly below $\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$: for $G=\mathbb Z/p$ over $\mathbb Q$ it gives $\mathrm{ed}^{[p-1]}\le1<\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Z/p)$ for $p\ge5$.

## 5. P4: divisibility of the jump degrees (the prime-to-$q$ tail)

Throughout this section $F'/F$ ranges over finite **separable** extensions. This is no restriction in characteristic $0$; in characteristic $p$ with $k$ perfect and $p\nmid|G|$ Lemma 5.2 reduces the general case to it.

**Definition/Lemma 5.1 ($q$-closure).** Let $q$ be a prime, $S\le\Gamma_F$ a Sylow pro-$q$ subgroup (exists, all conjugate [cited: Serre, *Galois cohomology*, I.1.4]) and $F^{(q)}=(F^{\rm sep})^S$. Then: (i) $F^{(q)}$ is the union of its finite subextensions, each of degree prime to $q$; (ii) every finite separable extension of $F^{(q)}$ has $q$-power degree; (iii) $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F^{(q)})$ does not depend on the choice of $S$; (iv) $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F^{(q)})$ [cited: Reichstein–Scavia (2.5)].

*Proof.* (i),(ii): $[F^{(q)}:F]=[\Gamma_F:S]$ is prime to $q$ as a supernatural number and $\Gamma_{F^{(q)}}=S$ is pro-$q$. (iii): if $S'=\gamma S\gamma^{-1}$ then $\gamma$ induces an $F$-isomorphism $F^{(q)}\to F'^{(q)}$ carrying $\tau\otimes F^{(q)}$ to $\tau\otimes F'^{(q)}$ and preserving subfields containing $k$ and their transcendence degrees. (iv): "$\le$": a descent of $\tau\otimes F^{(q)}$ to $F_0$ of transcendence degree $e$ involves finitely many elements of $F^{(q)}$, which lie in a finite subextension $F_1$, of degree prime to $q$ by (i); so $\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F_1)\le e$. "$\ge$": this is the next proposition. $\square$

**Proposition 5.2 (P4, first form).** If $F'/F$ is finite separable with $q\nmid[F':F]$, then $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')\ge\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F^{(q)})$.

*Proof.* $F'\otimes_FF^{(q)}$ is an étale $F^{(q)}$-algebra of dimension $[F':F]$, a product of finite separable extensions of $F^{(q)}$, each of $q$-power degree by 5.1(ii). If none had degree $1$, the sum of the degrees would be divisible by $q$; so some factor is $F^{(q)}$ itself, i.e. there is an $F$-embedding $F'\hookrightarrow F^{(q)}$. By (B1), $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')\ge\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F^{(q)})$. $\square$

(The naive argument "the compositum $F'F^{(q)}$ equals $F^{(q)}$" is false for a *given* embedding: for $F=\mathbb Q$, $q=2$, $F'=\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)$ one has $F'\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb Q^{(2)}\cong\mathbb Q^{(2)}\times\mathbb Q^{(2)}(\sqrt{-3})$, since $\sqrt{-3}\notin\mathbb Q^{(2)}$; one must choose the embedding.)

**Lemma 5.3 (purely inseparable invariance for $p\nmid|G|$).** Let $\operatorname{char}k=p$, $k$ perfect, $G$ finite of order prime to $p$ (or any finite constant $G$), $F'/F$ finite purely inseparable. Then $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)$. Hence $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)=\min\{\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F'):[F':F]\le d,\ F'/F\text{ separable}\}$, and Prop. 5.2 holds for all $F'$ with $q\nmid[F':F]$.

*Proof.* As in [P, Prop. 3.6]: $F'\subseteq F^{1/p^m}$, and $\sigma\colon F^{1/p^m}\to F$, $x\mapsto x^{p^m}$, is an isomorphism with $\sigma(k)=k$. Transport of structure gives $\mathrm{ed}_k(\sigma_*\tau')=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau')$ for torsors $\tau'$ over $F^{1/p^m}$. It remains to see $\sigma_*(\tau\otimes F^{1/p^m})\cong\tau$. Torsors under the constant group $G$ over a field $M$ correspond to continuous homomorphisms $\Gamma_M\to G$ up to conjugacy. Extend $\sigma$ to the $p^m$-power map $\tilde\sigma\colon F^{1/p^m}F^{\rm sep}\to F^{\rm sep}$ (the left side is a separable closure of $F^{1/p^m}$); $\tilde\sigma$ commutes with every automorphism, so the isomorphism $\Gamma_{F^{1/p^m}}\to\Gamma_F$ it induces is the canonical identification (restriction to $F^{\rm sep}$). Hence the classifying homomorphism of $\sigma_*(\tau\otimes F^{1/p^m})$ is $\varphi_\tau$. For the last statements: any finite $F'/F$ is purely inseparable over its separable closure $F'_s$ in $F'$, $[F'_s:F]\mid[F':F]$, and $\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F')=\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F'_s)$. $\square$

(Note this proof does not use $p\nmid|G|$; it applies to every finite constant $G$, and recovers [P, Prop. 3.6] without Witt vectors.)

**Proposition 5.4 (the generic torsor computes $\mathrm{ed}(G;q)$).** For every prime $q$, $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen};q)=\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$. [cited: Reichstein–Scavia, *The behavior of essential dimension under specialization*, and Merkurjev's survey (the statement "$\mathrm{ed}_p$ of a versal object equals $\mathrm{ed}_p$ of the functor"); proof sketch below, which is the standard "going down along a prime-to-$q$ correspondence" argument.]

*Proof sketch.* "$\le$" is the definition of $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$ as a supremum. For "$\ge$" let $e=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen};q)$ and let $\tau\colon T\to\operatorname{Spec}F$ be any $G$-torsor; we show $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)\le e$. If $F$ is finite, $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau)=0$; assume $F$ infinite. By Lemma 5.1(iv) there is a finite separable $K'/K$ of degree prime to $q$ and a descent $\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes K'\cong\tau_0\otimes_{K_0}K'$, $\operatorname{trdeg}_kK_0\le e$. Spread out: $\tau_0$ extends to a $G$-torsor $Z\to B$ over an integral $k$-variety $B$ of dimension $\le e$; $G$ acts freely on $Z$. Let $B'\to V/G$ be a finite dominant map of normal varieties with $k(B')=K'$ and $W'$ the normalisation of $V\times_{V/G}B'$; $W'$ is a $G$-variety, $W'\to V$ is finite dominant of degree $[K':K]$, and the descent gives a $G$-equivariant rational map $W'\dashrightarrow Z$ defined on a dense $G$-stable open $U\subseteq W'$. Shrinking, we may assume $W'\to V$ is finite étale of degree $[K':K]$ over a dense $G$-stable open $V_1\subseteq V$ with $U\supseteq W'\times_VV_1$. The twist ${}^\tau V$ is an $F$-form of $\mathbb A(V)$, hence isomorphic to affine space by Hilbert 90, so its $F$-points are dense; pick one in the dense open ${}^\tau V_1$. It corresponds (Fact 2.2(a) of [P], or §7 below) to a $G$-equivariant morphism $f\colon T\to V_1$. The pullback $T\times_{V}W'\to T$ is finite étale of degree $[K':K]$ and $G$-equivariant; as $T\to\operatorname{Spec}F$ is a $G$-torsor, $T\times_VW'\cong T\times_FA$ for a finite étale $F$-algebra $A$ of degree $[K':K]$ (Galois descent along $T\to\operatorname{Spec}F$). Some field factor $F'$ of $A$ has $q\nmid[F':F]$ (the degrees sum to $[K':K]$), and $T_{F'}=T\times_FF'\to T\times_VW'\to W'\times_VV_1\subseteq U\to Z$ is a $G$-equivariant morphism into the free $G$-variety $Z$. By [P, Lemma 2.3] (valid in any characteristic, §7), $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')\le\dim Z-\dim G=\dim B\le e$. Hence $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)\le e$. $\square$

**Theorem 5.5 (P4).** Let $\operatorname{char}k=0$, or $k$ perfect (Lemma 5.3). Let $\tau$ be a $G$-torsor over $F$ and $\tau_{\rm gen}$ the generic torsor.

(a) For every prime $q$ and every $j<\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)$, $q$ divides $d_j(\tau)$; equivalently, every $F'/F$ with $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')\le j$ has $q\mid[F':F]$.

(b) For $\tau_{\rm gen}$: every prime $q$ with $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)>j$ divides $d_j(\tau_{\rm gen})$, so
$$d_j(\tau_{\rm gen})\ \ge\ \prod_{q\ \text{prime},\ \mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)>j}q .$$

(c) For $\tau_{\rm gen}$ and every $d\ge1$:
$$\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})\ \ge\ \min_{1\le d'\le d}\ \max_{q\ \text{prime},\ q\nmid d'}\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)\ \ge\ \max_{q\ \text{prime},\ q>d}\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q).$$

(d) The prime-to-$q$ part of the profile, $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]\,(q')}_k(\tau):=\min\{\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F'):[F':F]\le d,\ q\nmid[F':F]\}$, is a non-increasing function with terminal value $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)$, reached at a finite prime-to-$q$ degree; for $\tau_{\rm gen}$ its terminal value is $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$.

*Proof.* (a) If $q\nmid[F':F]$ then $\mathrm{ed}(\tau\otimes F')\ge\mathrm{ed}(\tau;q)>j$ by Prop. 5.2. (b) is (a) with Prop. 5.4, and distinct primes dividing $d_j$ give the product bound. (c) A witness $F'$ for $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}$ has some degree $d'\le d$; for every prime $q\nmid d'$, $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')\ge\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$. Take the minimum over the possible $d'$. Since $q>d\ge d'$ implies $q\nmid d'$, the second inequality follows. (d) Lemma 5.1(iv) and Prop. 5.2, Prop. 5.4. $\square$

**Remark 5.6.** (i) In the language of [P, Thm 4.2/Rem. 4.5], (a) is exactly Reichstein–Scavia's Theorem 10.1 ("a $G$-equivariant correspondence of degree prime to $q$ from $Y$ to a smooth $X$ gives $\mathrm{ed}_q(X)\ge\mathrm{ed}_q(Y)$") applied to the correspondence $W'\rightsquigarrow Z$ furnished by a free closed point of degree prime to $q$; the proof above via the $q$-closure is the same mechanism. (ii) Consistency with [P] (characteristic $p$, $G=\mathbb Z/p^n$): there $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;p)\le1$ and $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)=0$ for $q\ne p$ (for $q\ne p$, $\tau\otimes F^{(q)}$ is split because $\Gamma_{F^{(q)}}$ is pro-$q$ and $G$ is a $p$-group, so $\mathrm{ed}(\tau;q)=0$), so Theorem 5.5 only constrains $j=0$: $p\mid d_0(\tau_{\rm gen})$, consistent with $d_0=p^n$; it says nothing about $d_1,\dots,d_{n-1}$, which is the point of [P]. (iii) In characteristic $0$ the constraint is much stronger, see P5 and the examples.

## 6. P5: $q$-groups in characteristic $\ne q$

**Lemma 6.1 [cited: Merkurjev, survey, Prop. on base change; Reichstein–Scavia Lemma 2.4].** For a finite extension $k'/k$ of degree prime to $q$ and any $\tau$ over $F\supseteq k$ with $k'\subseteq F$, $\mathrm{ed}_{k}(\tau;q)=\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(\tau;q)$; hence $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)=\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(G;q)$. (Sketch: "$\ge$" is (B2); "$\le$": a descent over $k'$ to $F_0\supseteq k'$ with $\operatorname{trdeg}_{k'}F_0=e$ after a prime-to-$q$ extension of $F$ is a descent over $k$ with $\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0=e$; so $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau;q)\le\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(\tau;q)$ when $k'\subseteq F$. If $k'\not\subseteq F$ pass to a composite $F'$, of degree prime to $q$ over $F$.)

**Proposition 6.2 (P5).** Let $G$ be a $q$-group of order $q^n$, $\operatorname{char}k\ne q$ ($k$ perfect if $\operatorname{char}k>0$), $c=[k(\mu_q):k]$ (a divisor of $q-1$). For $\tau_{\rm gen}$:

(a) $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})=\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$ for $c\le d\le q-1$, and $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)\le\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})\le\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$ for $1\le d<c$.

(b) $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)=\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_q)}(G)=$ the minimal dimension of a faithful representation of $G$ over $k(\mu_q)$ [cited: Karpenko–Merkurjev].

(c) For every $j<\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$, $q\mid d_j(\tau_{\rm gen})$; every $F'/K$ of degree prime to $q$ has $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')\ge\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$. $d_0(\tau_{\rm gen})=q^n$.

(d) If $\mu_q\subset k$ ($c=1$): $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$ for all $d<q$, and $d_j(\tau_{\rm gen})\in q\mathbb Z$ for all $j<\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$: **the whole profile of $\tau_{\rm gen}$ is a step function all of whose steps occur at multiples of $q$**, i.e. $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=\mathrm{ed}^{[q\lfloor d/q\rfloor]}(\tau_{\rm gen})$ for $d\ge q$.

*Proof.* (a) Lower bound: every extension of degree $\le d<q$ has degree prime to $q$, so Prop. 5.2 and 5.4 give $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}\ge\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$. Upper bound for $d\ge c$: Cor. 4.2 gives $\mathrm{ed}^{[c]}\le\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_q)}(G)$, which equals $\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_q)}(G;q)$ by Karpenko–Merkurjev (their theorem gives $\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(G)=\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(G;q)$ for $\mu_q\subset k'$) and equals $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$ by Lemma 6.1 ($c\mid q-1$ is prime to $q$). (b) is the same chain. (c) Theorem 5.5(b), and P2(d). (d) is (a)+(c) with $c=1$ and $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)=\mathrm{ed}_k(G)$. $\square$

**Remark 6.3.** (i) Compare [P, Prop. 3.7]: in characteristic $p$ the prime-to-$p$ tail of a $p$-group collapses to $\le1$; in characteristic $\ne q$ the prime-to-$q$ tail of a $q$-group is constant equal to $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$ — the two situations are opposite extremes. (ii) The statement (d) for $j\ge1$ says nothing about *which* multiples of $q$ occur; for $(\mathbb Z/2)^n$ see E2, where $d_{n-1}=2$, $d_{n-2}=4$, $d_0=2^n$ and the intermediate $d_j$ are only known to be even and $\le 2^{n-j}$.

## 7. P6: the geometric characterisation in every characteristic; induced torsors

**Theorem 7.1 ([P, Thm 4.2] for arbitrary finite $G$, arbitrary $k$, any characteristic).** Let $\tau\colon T\to\operatorname{Spec}F$ be a $G$-torsor, $F\supseteq k$, $m,d\ge0$. The following are equivalent:

1. $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}_k(\tau)\le m$;
2. there is a quasi-projective generically free primitive $G$-variety $Z$ over $k$ with $\dim Z\le m$ such that the twist ${}^\tau Z$ has a closed point of degree $\le d$ in the free locus ${}^\tau(Z^{\rm free})$;
3. there are $Z$ as in (2) and $F'/F$ with $[F':F]\le d$ and a $G$-equivariant morphism $T\times_FF'\to Z^{\rm free}$.

Consequently $d_j(\tau)=\min\{\delta_{\rm free}(Z,\tau):\dim Z\le j\}$ with $\delta_{\rm free}$ the minimal degree of a free closed point of ${}^\tau Z$ ([P, Cor. 4.4]).

*Why the proof of [P] goes through.* The proof in [P] uses four ingredients, none of which involves the characteristic or the structure of $G$:

* **Twists.** For $G$ finite (constant) and $Z$ quasi-projective with $G$-action, $T\times_kZ$ is quasi-projective over $F$ with a free $G$-action, so the quotient ${}^\tau Z=(T\times_kZ)/G$ exists as a quasi-projective $F$-scheme (SGA 1, V.1; equivalently Galois descent along the finite Galois algebra $T/F$); it commutes with base change in $F$ and takes $G$-stable opens to opens [cited: Duncan–Reichstein, *Versality…*, §3; the construction there is over an arbitrary field].
* **Points of the twist = equivariant maps** ($F$-points of ${}^\tau Z$ are $\operatorname{Hom}_G(T,Z_F)$, [P, Fact 2.2(a)]): elementary for finite $G$: the fibre of $T\times_kZ\to{}^\tau Z$ over an $F$-point $x$ is a $G$-torsor $P_x$ over $F$ with a $G$-equivariant map $P_x\to T$, which is an isomorphism (an equivariant map of torsors is), giving $T\cong P_x\to Z$; conversely the graph of an equivariant $T\to Z$ is a $G$-stable closed $T\subseteq T\times Z$ whose image is an $F$-point. Free points correspond to maps into $Z^{\rm free}$ because ${}^\tau(Z^{\rm free})\subseteq{}^\tau Z$ is the open subscheme whose $F'$-points are the maps $T_{F'}\to Z^{\rm free}$ (compatibility with base change and opens).
* **Free point $\Rightarrow$ descent** ([P, Lemma 2.3]): $G$ acts freely on the quasi-projective $Z^{\rm free}$ (here $Z^{\rm free}=Z\smallsetminus\bigcup_{1\ne h\in G}Z^h$ with $Z^h$ the fixed-point *subscheme*, so the action is free in the scheme sense), hence $\pi\colon Z^{\rm free}\to B:=Z^{\rm free}/G$ is a $G$-torsor (SGA 1, V, Cor. 2.4: finite group acting freely $\Rightarrow$ étale quotient, in any characteristic). An equivariant $f\colon T_{F'}\to Z^{\rm free}$ induces $\bar f\colon\operatorname{Spec}F'\to B$ with $\bar f^*\pi\cong\tau\otimes F'$; if $b$ is the image point, $\tau\otimes F'$ descends to $\kappa(b)$ with $\operatorname{trdeg}_k\kappa(b)\le\dim B=\dim Z\le m$.
* **Spreading out** ((1)$\Rightarrow$(3)): a descent $\tau\otimes F'\cong\tau_0\otimes_{F_0}F'$ with $F_0$ finitely generated over $k$ (shrink $F_0$ to the field generated by finitely many structure constants) spreads out to a $G$-torsor $Z\to B$ over an integral affine $k$-variety $B$ with $k(B)=F_0$; $Z$ is affine, $G$ acts freely, and $Z$ is primitive since its generic fibre is a torsor over a field. The composite $T_{F'}\to T_0\to Z$ is equivariant.

(2)$\Leftrightarrow$(3) is the identification of closed points of degree $\le d$ with $F'$-points, $[F':F]\le d$, exactly as in [P]. $\square$

**Remark 7.2.** For $\tau=\tau_{\rm gen}$ (any faithful $V$) the supplement of [P, Thm 4.2] also holds: an $F'$-point as in (3) is a $G$-equivariant rational map $W'\dashrightarrow Z^{\rm free}$ from the normalisation $W'$ of $V$ in $L\otimes_KF'$, i.e. a $G$-equivariant correspondence $V\rightsquigarrow Z$ of degree $[F':K]\le d$ in the sense of Reichstein–Scavia landing in the free locus. So the profile of $\tau_{\rm gen}$ is the function
$$d\mapsto\min\{\dim Z:\ \exists\ G\text{-correspondence }V\rightsquigarrow Z\text{ of degree}\le d\text{ into }Z^{\rm free}\},$$
and the jump degrees are the minimal degrees of such correspondences into free $G$-varieties of dimension $\le j$. Theorem 5.5 is the statement that a correspondence of degree prime to $q$ cannot lower $\mathrm{ed}(\,\cdot\,;q)$.

**Lemma 7.3 (induced torsors; extends [P, Prop. 3.9] to non-abelian $G$, any characteristic, any $k$).** Let $H\le G$ be finite groups and $\tau_H\colon T_H\to\operatorname{Spec}F$ an $H$-torsor. Then $\mathrm{ed}_k(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\tau_H)=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_H)$.

*Proof.* "$\le$": descend $\tau_H$ to $F_0$ and induce; induction commutes with base change. "$\ge$": first reduce to $T_H$ connected: if $T_H=\operatorname{Ind}_{H'}^H T'$ with $T'$ a field, then $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\tau_H=\operatorname{Ind}_{H'}^G\tau'$; the "$\le$" direction for $(H',H)$ gives $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_H)\le\mathrm{ed}(\tau')$, and the connected case for $(H',G)$ gives $\mathrm{ed}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H'}^G\tau')\ge\mathrm{ed}(\tau')$; together $\mathrm{ed}(\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\tau_H)\ge\mathrm{ed}(\tau')\ge\mathrm{ed}(\tau_H)$. So let $T_H$ be a field. Let $T_0\to\operatorname{Spec}F_0$, $F_0\subseteq F$, be a descent of $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\tau_H$, $T'_0$ a connected component of $T_0$ with stabiliser $H_0$; $T'_0$ is a field, $H_0$-Galois over $F_0$, and $T_0\otimes_{F_0}F\cong\coprod_{G/H_0}T'_0\otimes_{F_0}F$. The components of $T_0\otimes F\cong\coprod_{G/H}T_H$ are the $gT_H$, with stabilisers $gHg^{-1}$; the components of $T'_0\otimes_{F_0}F$ are among them and are permuted transitively by $H_0$, so their stabilisers are conjugates of $H$ contained in $H_0$. Replacing $H$ by a conjugate (which changes $\tau_H$ by transport of structure and does not change $\mathrm{ed}$ or $\operatorname{Ind}$), we may assume $T_H\subseteq T'_0\otimes_{F_0}F$ and $H\le H_0$. Put $F_1=(T'_0)^H$, a finite extension of $F_0$ inside $T'_0$, so $\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_1=\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0$, and $T'_0/F_1$ is an $H$-torsor. Write $F_1\otimes_{F_0}F=\prod_iF_i$ (fields); then $T'_0\otimes_{F_0}F=\prod_iT'_0\otimes_{F_1}F_i$, each factor an $H$-torsor over the field $F_i$, so $H$ acts transitively on its components. The component $T_H$ lies in one factor $T'_0\otimes_{F_1}F_{i_0}$ and is $H$-stable, so that factor equals $T_H$; taking $H$-invariants, $F_{i_0}=T_H^H=F$. Thus $F_1\to F_{i_0}=F$ is an $F_0$-embedding and $\tau_H\cong(T'_0/F_1)\otimes_{F_1}F$ descends to $F_1$. $\square$

**Corollary 7.4.** If $\tau\otimes F'\cong\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\tau_H$ for some $H$-torsor $\tau_H$ over $F'$, then $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes F')=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_H)\le\mathrm{ed}_k(H)$. This applies in particular to the intermediate fields of the splitting field: over the fixed field $F'=E^{H'}$ of a subgroup $H'\le\operatorname{im}\varphi_\tau$ (with $E$ the splitting field), $\tau\otimes F'\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{H'}^{G}(E/F')$, so $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau\otimes E^{H'})=\mathrm{ed}_k(E/E^{H'})\le\mathrm{ed}_k(H')$ and $\mathrm{ed}^{[[H:H']]}_k(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}_k(H')$.

*Proof.* $T\otimes_FF'$ with $F'=E^{H'}$: $E\otimes_FE^{H'}\cong\prod_{H/H'}E$ and the $H$-action permutes the factors through $H/H'$, so $T\otimes_FF'\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{H'}^G(E/F')$. $\square$

This is the general form of the "root field" reductions used for $S_n$ in E4 and of [P, Prop. 3.9]; it gives the universal upper bound
$$\mathrm{ed}^{[[G:H']]}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})\le\mathrm{ed}_k(H')\quad\text{for every subgroup }H'\le G,$$
and in fact with equality, because $L/L^{H'}$ is the generic fibre of $V\to V/H'$, i.e. the generic $H'$-torsor of the faithful $H'$-representation $V|_{H'}$ over the same base field $k$ (this is how [P, Prop. 3.9] and the root-field reductions of E4 arise).

**Corollary 7.5 (subgroup profile).** For every finite $G$ and every subgroup $H'\le G$: $\mathrm{ed}^{[[G:H']]}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})\le\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes L^{H'})=\mathrm{ed}_k(H')$. Hence $d_j(\tau_{\rm gen})\le\min\{[G:H']:\mathrm{ed}_k(H')\le j\}$; e.g. $d_0(\tau_{\rm gen})\le|G|$ ($H'=1$), $d_1(\tau_{\rm gen})\le\min\{[G:C]:C\text{ cyclic},\ \mu_{|C|}\subset k\}$ in characteristic $0$.

*Proof.* $L/L^{H'}$ is the generic fibre of $V\to V/H'$, the generic $H'$-torsor of $V|_{H'}$, so $\mathrm{ed}_k(L/L^{H'})=\mathrm{ed}_k(H')$ [cited: independence of the generic torsor from $V$, Berhuy–Favi/Merkurjev]; apply Lemma 7.3. $\square$

## 8. Worked examples

### E1. $G=\mathbb Z/n$, $\mu_n\subset k$, $\operatorname{char}k\nmid n$ [proved here]

Take $V=\mathbb A^1$ with a faithful character: $L=k(t)$, $K=k(a)$, $a=t^n$, $\tau_{\rm gen}=K(\sqrt[n]a)/K$. Then $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})\le1$, and $\ge1$ because $\tau_{\rm gen}$ does not descend to the algebraic closure of $k$ in $K$, which is $k$ (a torsor from $k$ has constant splitting field $k\cdot l$, whereas the splitting field $k(t)$ of $\tau_{\rm gen}$ is regular over $k$; P2(c) with $[F':K]=1<n$). By P2(d), $d_0(\tau_{\rm gen})=n$. Hence the profile is
$$\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=1\ (1\le d\le n-1),\qquad \mathrm{ed}^{[n]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=0 .$$
For an arbitrary $\mathbb Z/n$-torsor $\tau$ over $F$ with $|\operatorname{im}\varphi_\tau|=m\mid n$: $\mathrm{ed}\le1$ and $d_0(\tau)\le m$, with equality if $k$ is algebraically closed. Consistent with Theorem 5.5: $\mathrm{ed}_k(\mathbb Z/n;q)=1$ for every $q\mid n$, so every prime divisor of $n$ divides $d_0$.

### E2. $G=(\mathbb Z/2)^n$, $k$ algebraically closed, $\operatorname{char}k\ne2$

$F=K=k(a_1,\dots,a_n)$, $\tau_{\rm gen}=F(\sqrt{a_1},\dots,\sqrt{a_n})$, class $(a_1,\dots,a_n)\in H^1(F,\mathbb Z/2)^n$. Write $(a_I)=\bigcup_{i\in I}(a_i)\in H^{|I|}(F,\mathbb Z/2)$ for $I\subseteq[n]$. Coefficients are always $\mathbb Z/2$; since $-1\in k^{\times2}$, $(x)\cup(x)=(x)\cup(-1)=0$.

**Facts used.** (F1) $\mathrm{ed}_k((\mathbb Z/2)^n)=n$ [cited: Buhler–Reichstein]; the lower bound is also a consequence of (F3) below since $(a_{[n]})\ne0$. (F2) (Serre) if $F_0\supseteq k$ has $\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0\le m$ then $\operatorname{cd}(F_0)\le m$, so $H^{m+1}(F_0,\mathbb Z/2)=0$ [cited: Serre, *Galois cohomology*, II.4.2, Prop. 11]. (F3) For $F_m=k(a_1,\dots,a_m)$ and the $a_m$-adic valuation with residue field $F_{m-1}$, the residue map $\partial\colon H^r(F_m)\to H^{r-1}(F_{m-1})$ satisfies $\partial(x\cup(a_m))=\bar x$ and $\partial(x)=0$ for $x$ coming from unit classes; by induction $(a_{[m]})\ne0$ in $H^m(F_m)$ and more generally $(a_I)\ne0$ for all $I$. (F4) (Arason sequence) For $f\in F^\times\smallsetminus F^{\times2}$ and every $r\ge1$ the sequence
$$H^{r-1}(F)\xrightarrow{(f)\cup}H^r(F)\xrightarrow{\rm res}H^r(F(\sqrt f))$$
is exact [cited: Arason 1975, Satz 4.5]. Derivation: with $\Gamma'=\Gamma_{F(\sqrt f)}$ of index $2$ in $\Gamma=\Gamma_F$, the sequence of $\Gamma$-modules $0\to\mathbb Z/2\to\mathbb Z/2[\Gamma/\Gamma']\to\mathbb Z/2\to0$ (the augmentation, which splits as $\mathbb Z/2$-vector spaces but not as modules) gives, via Shapiro, the long exact sequence $\cdots\to H^{r-1}(F)\xrightarrow{\delta}H^r(F)\xrightarrow{\rm res}H^r(F(\sqrt f))\to\cdots$, and the connecting map $\delta$ is the cup product with the class of the extension $0\to\mathbb Z/2\to\mathbb Z/2[\Gamma/\Gamma']\to\mathbb Z/2\to0$ in $H^1(F,\mathbb Z/2)=\operatorname{Ext}^1$, which is $(f)$. (F5) (complete fields) For $\hat F=F_{n-1}((a_n))$: $H^r(\hat F)=H^r(F_{n-1})\oplus H^{r-1}(F_{n-1})\cup(a_n)$, the first summand being the image of inflation from the residue field; units $u\in F_{n-1}[[a_n]]^\times$ satisfy $(u)=(\bar u)$ [cited: Garibaldi–Merkurjev–Serre, *Cohomological invariants*, §7; Serre, II.4.3].

**Proposition 8.1.** Let $n\ge1$. (a) $\mathrm{ed}^{[2^j]}(\tau_{\rm gen})\le n-j$ for $0\le j\le n$, and $d_0=2^n$. (b) Every $F'/F$ of odd degree has $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')=n$; all $d_j$, $j<n$, are even. (c) For **every** quadratic $F'=F(\sqrt f)$, $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')\ge n-1$; hence $\mathrm{ed}^{[2]}=\mathrm{ed}^{[3]}=n-1$, $d_{n-1}=2$, and $d_{n-2}=4$ for $n\ge2$. (d) $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}=1$ for $2^{n-1}\le d<2^n$. (e) Complete profiles: $n=1$: $(1,0)$. $n=2$: $(2,1,1,0)$, $d_1=2$, $d_0=4$. $n=3$: $(3,2,2,1,1,1,1,0)$, $d_2=2$, $d_1=4$, $d_0=8$. [proved here]

*Proof.* (a) Over $F_j'=F(\sqrt{a_1},\dots,\sqrt{a_j})$ the class is $(1,\dots,1,a_{j+1},\dots,a_n)$, which descends to $k(a_{j+1},\dots,a_n)$; $d_0=2^n$ is P2. (b) $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;2)=\mathrm{ed}_k(G)=n$ (Prop. 6.2(b)); apply Prop. 5.2 and Theorem 5.5(a). (d) $\mathrm{ed}^{[2^{n-1}]}\le1$ by (a) and $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}\ge1$ for $d<2^n$ by P2. (e) follows from (a)–(d): for $n=3$, $\mathrm{ed}^{[1]}=3$, $\mathrm{ed}^{[2]}=\mathrm{ed}^{[3]}=2$ by (c), $\mathrm{ed}^{[4]}\le1$ by (a) and $\ge1$ by P2, $\mathrm{ed}^{[8]}=0$.

(c) Suppose $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')\le n-2$ for $F'=F(\sqrt f)$, $f\notin F^{\times2}$. Then $(a_1,\dots,a_n)_{F'}$ descends to $F_0\subseteq F'$ with $\operatorname{trdeg}_kF_0\le n-2$, and by functoriality of cup products, for every $I$ with $|I|=n-1$, $(a_I)_{F'}$ is the restriction of a class in $H^{n-1}(F_0)=0$ (F2). By (F4), $(a_I)\in(f)\cup H^{n-2}(F)$ for all $I=[n]\smallsetminus\{i\}$. The following claim gives the contradiction.

**Claim $C(m)$.** For $m\ge1$, $F_m=k(a_1,\dots,a_m)$, there is no $g\in F_m^\times$ with $(a_{[m]\smallsetminus i})\in(g)\cup H^{m-2}(F_m)$ for all $i\in[m]$ (with $H^{-1}=0$).

*Proof of the claim by induction on $m$.* $m=1$: $(a_\emptyset)=1\in H^0(F_1)=\mathbb Z/2$ is nonzero while $(g)\cup H^{-1}=0$. Let $m\ge2$ and suppose $g$ exists. Work in $\hat F=F_{m-1}((a_m))\supseteq F_m$ and use (F5); restriction to $\hat F$ is injective on the classes $(a_I)$ (their $\hat F$-components are $(a_{I\smallsetminus m})\ne0$ in the appropriate summand by (F3)). Modulo squares $g=a_m^\varepsilon u$ with $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$ and $u$ a unit; $(u)=(\bar u)$ with $\bar u\in F_{m-1}^\times$. Write the given $x_i\in H^{m-2}(F_m)$ as $x_i=y_i+z_i\cup(a_m)$ in $H^{m-2}(\hat F)$, $y_i\in H^{m-2}(F_{m-1})$, $z_i\in H^{m-3}(F_{m-1})$.

*Case $\varepsilon=1$.* Take $i=m$: $(a_{[m-1]})=(a_mu)\cup x_m=(\bar u)\cup y_i+\big(y_m+(\bar u)\cup z_m\big)\cup(a_m)$ (using $(a_m)\cup(a_m)=0$). Comparing the two summands of (F5): $y_m=(\bar u)\cup z_m$ and $(a_{[m-1]})=(\bar u)\cup y_m=(\bar u)\cup(\bar u)\cup z_m=0$ in $H^{m-1}(F_{m-1})$, contradicting (F3).

*Case $\varepsilon=0$.* For $i<m$: $(a_{[m]\smallsetminus i})=(a_{[m-1]\smallsetminus i})\cup(a_m)$, and $(\bar u)\cup x_i=(\bar u)\cup y_i+\big((\bar u)\cup z_i\big)\cup(a_m)$. Comparing the second summands: $(a_{[m-1]\smallsetminus i})=(\bar u)\cup z_i\in(\bar u)\cup H^{m-3}(F_{m-1})$ for all $i\in[m-1]$, contradicting $C(m-1)$ with $g=\bar u$. $\square$

Hence no quadratic extension lowers $\mathrm{ed}$ by two, i.e. $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')\in\{n-1,n\}$ for every quadratic $F'$; with (a) and (b) this gives $\mathrm{ed}^{[2]}=\mathrm{ed}^{[3]}=n-1$ and $d_{n-1}=2$; $d_{n-2}>3$ and $d_{n-2}\le4$ by (a), so $d_{n-2}=4$ (and, being even, no cubic or quintic extension can compete anyway). $\square$

**Remark 8.2 (what is open for $(\mathbb Z/2)^n$).** (i) $d_{n-2}=4$ is attained by biquadratic extensions; whether a non-biquadratic quartic (cyclic, or non-Galois, quartic with Galois group $D_4$, $A_4$, $S_4$) can lower $\mathrm{ed}$ by $3$ is open for $n\ge4$; for $n=3$ it cannot (splitting needs degree $8$). The first open slot is $n=4$: $\mathrm{ed}^{[4]}(\tau_{\rm gen})\in\{1,2\}$; the value $1$ would require a quartic $F'/F$ over which all six quaternion algebras $(a_i,a_j)$ split (since $\mathrm{ed}\le1$ forces $H^2$-invariants to vanish, (F2)). The kernel of $H^2(F)\to H^2(F')$ for a non-Galois quartic $F'$ has no description as clean as (F4), which is exactly what makes $\mathrm{ed}^{[4]}$ hard. (ii) Conjecturally $\mathrm{ed}^{[2^j]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=n-j$ and $d_j=2^{n-j}$ for all $j$; proved here for $j\in\{0,1,n-1,n\}$ and for all $n\le3$. [open for $2\le j\le n-2$] (iii) The method of (c) does not obviously extend to towers of quadratic extensions: after the first layer the Arason sequence lives over a field that is no longer purely transcendental over $k$, and the residue induction (F5) needs the valuations $a_i=0$ to stay well behaved. Whether $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')\ge n-r$ for every multiquadratic $F'$ of degree $2^r$ is [open] for $2\le r\le n-2$. (iv) The coincidence with [P]: the profile $(2,1,1,0)$ of the generic $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$-torsor in characteristic $\ne2$ equals that of the generic $\mathbb Z/4$-torsor in characteristic $2$ ([P, Thm 6.2]); both are forced by $\mathrm{ed}=2$, $d_1=2$, $d_0=4$, so this is not deep.

### E3. $G=\mathbb Z/p$ over $k=\mathbb Q$ (no $\mu_p$)

Let $p$ be an odd prime, $\tau$ any $\mathbb Z/p$-torsor over $F\supseteq\mathbb Q$, $c=[F(\mu_p):F]\mid p-1$.

* By Cor. 4.2, $\mathrm{ed}^{[c]}_{\mathbb Q}(\tau)\le\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q(\mu_p)}(\mathbb Z/p)=1$ (E1). So $d_1(\tau)\le c\le p-1$ for **every** $\mathbb Z/p$-torsor over every field containing $\mathbb Q$. [proved here]
* On the other hand $\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Z/p)\ge2$ for $p\ge5$ [cited from memory: Ledet, *Finite groups of essential dimension one*, J. Algebra 2007, cited as [6] in [P]: for $k$ of characteristic $0$, $\mathrm{ed}_k(G)=1$ iff $G\ne1$ embeds in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)$; and $\mathbb Z/p\not\hookrightarrow\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q)$ for $p\ge5$: an element of order $p$ lifts to $A\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Q)$ with eigenvalues $\lambda,\lambda\zeta$, $\zeta$ a primitive $p$-th root of unity, and $\operatorname{tr}(A)^2/\det A=\zeta+2+\zeta^{-1}\in\mathbb Q$ forces $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\in\mathbb Q$, i.e. $p\le3$. Independently, $\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Z/5)=2$ and $\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Z/7)=2$ are, I believe, in Buhler–Reichstein / Ledet, and the upper bound $\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Z/p)\le(p-1)/2$ is classical; all [cited from memory, to be checked].]
* Hence for $\tau_{\rm gen}$ over $\mathbb Q$ and $p\ge5$: $\mathrm{ed}^{[1]}\ge2$, $\mathrm{ed}^{[p-1]}\le1$, $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}\ge1$ for $d<p$ (P2), $\mathrm{ed}^{[p]}=0$. The profile drops to $1$ at a degree dividing $p-1$, i.e. **prime to $p$**, which is consistent with Theorem 5.5 because $\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathbb Z/p;p)=\mathrm{ed}_{\mathbb Q(\mu_p)}(\mathbb Z/p;p)=1$ (Lemma 6.1): only $d_0$ is constrained to be a multiple of $p$.
* Finer question: which divisor of $p-1$ is $d_1(\tau_{\rm gen})$? By Cor. 4.2 with $k'$ any subfield of $\mathbb Q(\mu_p)$, $d_1\le\min\{[k':\mathbb Q]:k'\subseteq\mathbb Q(\mu_p),\ \mathrm{ed}_{k'}(\mathbb Z/p)=1\}$. If Ledet's criterion is as recalled, $\mathrm{ed}_{k'}(\mathbb Z/p)=1$ iff $\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}\in k'$ (the computation above runs backwards: if $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\in k'$ the matrix with characteristic polynomial $x^2-(2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1})x+(2+\zeta+\zeta^{-1})$ has eigenvalue ratio $\zeta$), so the natural guess is $d_1(\tau_{\rm gen})=[\mathbb Q(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}):\mathbb Q]=(p-1)/2$, e.g. $d_1=2$ for $p=5$ with witness $K(\sqrt5)$, giving the profile $(2,1,1,1,0)$ for the generic $\mathbb Z/5$-torsor over $\mathbb Q$. The lower bound $d_1(\tau_{\rm gen})\ge(p-1)/2$ (no extension of $\mathbb Q(a)$ of smaller degree makes the generic $\mathbb Z/p$-torsor one-dimensional) is [open]; it is not implied by Theorem 5.5.

### E4. Symmetric groups $S_n$, $\operatorname{char}k=0$

$V=k^n$ with the permutation action; $L=k(x_1,\dots,x_n)$, $K=F=k(s_1,\dots,s_n)$ (elementary symmetric functions); $\tau_{\rm gen}$ is the generic degree-$n$ étale algebra $F[x]/(x^n-s_1x^{n-1}+\dots\pm s_n)$.

**Proposition 8.3.** For $0\le m\le n$ let $F_m=F(x_1,\dots,x_m)$, $[F_m:F]=n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)$. Then
$$\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F_m)=\mathrm{ed}_k(S_{n-m}),\qquad\text{hence }\mathrm{ed}^{[n(n-1)\cdots(n-m+1)]}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})\le\mathrm{ed}_k(S_{n-m}).$$
[proved here]

*Proof.* $F_m=L^{S_{n-m}}$ where $S_{n-m}$ permutes $x_{m+1},\dots,x_n$, so by Cor. 7.4/7.5 $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F_m)=\mathrm{ed}_k(L/F_m)$. Now $L/F_m$ is the generic $S_{n-m}$-torsor of the permutation representation $k^{n-m}$ pulled back along the purely transcendental extension $k(s'_1,\dots,s'_{n-m})\subseteq k(s'_1,\dots,s'_{n-m})(x_1,\dots,x_m)=F_m$ ($s'_i$ the elementary symmetric functions of $x_{m+1},\dots,x_n$; $x_1,\dots,x_m$ are algebraically independent over $k(s')$ for degree reasons). By (B4) (purely transcendental base change does not change $\mathrm{ed}$; $F$ infinite) $\mathrm{ed}_k(L/F_m)=\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen}(S_{n-m}))=\mathrm{ed}_k(S_{n-m})$. $\square$

**Lower bounds.** $\mathrm{ed}_k(S_n;q)=\lfloor n/q\rfloor$ for every prime $q$ [cited: Meyer–Reichstein, *Some consequences of the Karpenko–Merkurjev theorem*, Doc. Math. 2010; the lower bound via the elementary abelian subgroup $(\mathbb Z/q)^{\lfloor n/q\rfloor}$, the upper bound via $\mathrm{ed}(S_n;q)\le\mathrm{ed}$ of a Sylow]. Theorem 5.5 then gives, for $\tau_{\rm gen}=\tau_{\rm gen}(S_n)$ over any $k$ of characteristic $0$:
$$\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}(\tau_{\rm gen})\ \ge\ \min_{d'\le d}\ \max_{q\nmid d'}\lfloor n/q\rfloor\ \ge\ \lfloor n/q_d\rfloor,\quad q_d=\text{the least prime}>d,$$
and $q\mid d_j(\tau_{\rm gen})$ whenever $\lfloor n/q\rfloor>j$. By Bertrand, $q_d<2d+2$, so $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}(\tau_{\rm gen}(S_n))\gtrsim n/(2d+2)$: **the profile of the generic étale algebra of degree $n$ decays no faster than $n/(2d)$**, whereas the upper bound of Prop. 8.3 needs degree $\sim n^m$ to lower the value by $m$. The true growth of $d_j(\tau_{\rm gen}(S_n))$ in $n$ is [open]; the two bounds are $\approx n/(j+1)\le d_j\le n!/(n-m_j)!$ with $m_j$ minimal such that $\mathrm{ed}_k(S_{n-m_j})\le j$.

## 9. Discussion: characteristic $0$ versus characteristic $p$

The two regimes are governed by different mechanisms.

**Characteristic $0$ (or prime to $|G|$).** The profile of $\tau_{\rm gen}$ is squeezed between two rigid constraints:

1. *Sylow divisibility* (Theorem 5.5): a prime $q$ with $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)>j$ divides every jump degree $d_i$, $i\le j$. Since $\mathrm{ed}_k(G;q)$ is a Sylow invariant computed by Karpenko–Merkurjev (after adjoining $\mu_q$), the divisibility pattern of the jump degrees is determined by the Sylow subgroups, and the prime-to-$q$ tail of the profile is *constant*. For a $q$-group with $\mu_q\subset k$, the profile only moves at multiples of $q$ (Prop. 6.2(d)).
2. *Roots-of-unity defect* (Cor. 4.2): within degree $[k(\mu_e):k]$ the profile drops to at most $\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G)$, a representation-theoretic quantity. Everything above that value in the profile is "arithmetic of $k$", concentrated in degrees dividing $\varphi(e)$, hence prime to $|G|$ when $|G|$ is a prime power. In particular over an algebraically closed $k$ this layer is absent and the profile starts at the representation-theoretic value.
3. *Subgroup upper bounds* (Cor. 7.5): $\mathrm{ed}^{[[G:H']]}(\tau_{\rm gen})\le\mathrm{ed}_k(H')$; over $L^{H'}$ the value is exactly $\mathrm{ed}_k(H')$. The examples suggest these are far from optimal in general ($S_n$: degree $\sim n^m$ for a drop of $m$, versus lower bounds $\sim n/d$) but optimal in the elementary abelian $2$-group case for the first drop.

What remains unknown in characteristic $0$ is whether *non-Galois or non-subgroup* extensions can beat the subgroup witnesses: for $(\mathbb Z/2)^n$ this is the question about quartic non-biquadratic extensions, for $S_6$ about quadratic non-discriminant and sextic non-root extensions. Cohomological invariants (used in E2) detect the first drop only, because the kernel of restriction along a quadratic extension is controlled (Arason), while kernels along higher-degree or non-Galois extensions are not.

**Characteristic $p$, $G$ a $p$-group** ([P]). There is no Sylow divisibility beyond $p\mid d_0$ ($\mathrm{ed}(G;p)\le1$), no roots-of-unity layer, and the whole profile lives in the $p$-power degrees: it records the Artin–Schreier(–Witt) layers that must be adjoined to reach the Teichmüller form, and it is invisible to $\mathrm{ed}(\,\cdot\,;q)$ for all $q$ and to all large-field invariants. The one common ingredient is the "root-field/subgroup" upper bound: $\mathrm{ed}^{[p^j]}(\tau_{\rm gen})\le n-j$ in [P, Prop. 3.4] is the analogue of Prop. 8.1(a) here (adjoin $j$ generators), and [P, Prop. 3.9] is Cor. 7.5 for $\mathbb Z/p^{n-j}\le\mathbb Z/p^n$.

**A common framework.** Theorem 7.1 shows that in both regimes the profile is the minimal degree of a $G$-correspondence into a free $G$-variety of bounded dimension. Theorem 5.5 identifies the part of that minimal degree that is forced by Reichstein–Scavia's prime-to-$q$ correspondence theorem; the remaining part — the $q$-power part in characteristic $\ne q$, the $p$-power part in characteristic $p$ — is where the profile carries information beyond $\mathrm{ed}$ and $\mathrm{ed}(\,\cdot\,;q)$.

## 10. Open questions

**(Q1) Optimality of the elementary abelian tower.** For $G=(\mathbb Z/2)^n$ over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $\ne2$, is $\mathrm{ed}^{[2^j]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=n-j$, equivalently $d_j=2^{n-j}$, for all $j$? Known for $j\in\{0,1,n-1,n\}$ (Prop. 8.1). The first open case is $n=4$, $j=2$: is there a quartic extension of $k(a_1,\dots,a_4)$ splitting all six quaternion algebras $(a_i,a_j)$? The same question for $(\mathbb Z/q)^n$ with $\mu_q\subset k$: is $d_j=q^{n-j}$? Here already $\mathrm{ed}^{[q]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=n-1$ (no extension of degree $\le q$ lowers $\mathrm{ed}$ by two) needs the analogue of (F4) for cyclic extensions of degree $q$ with $\mu_q\subset k$ and $\mathbb Z/q$-coefficients: $\ker\big(H^r(F)\to H^r(F(\sqrt[q]f))\big)=(f)\cup H^{r-1}(F)$, a standard fact [cited from memory: Arason; Gille–Szamuely, *Central simple algebras and Galois cohomology*, the exact sequence for cyclic extensions] whose derivation for $q>2$ is not the two-term augmentation argument of (F4). Granting it, the residue induction of Prop. 8.1(c) goes through verbatim and gives $\mathrm{ed}(\tau_{\rm gen}\otimes F')\ge n-1$ for every cyclic $F'$ of degree $q$; since all other degrees in $[1,2q)$ are prime to $q$ (Theorem 5.5), this yields $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}(\tau_{\rm gen})=n-1$ for $q\le d<2q$ and $d_{n-1}=q$. [expected], not written out.

**(Q2) The roots-of-unity layer.** For $G=\mathbb Z/p$ over $\mathbb Q$, is $d_1(\tau_{\rm gen})=[\mathbb Q(\zeta_p+\zeta_p^{-1}):\mathbb Q]=(p-1)/2$? More generally, for which $G$ and $k$ is $d_{\mathrm{ed}_{k(\mu_e)}(G)}(\tau_{\rm gen})=[k(\mu_e):k]$, i.e. is Cor. 4.2 sharp, and can a *non-constant* extension (one not of the form $Fk'$) do better than adjoining roots of unity?

**(Q3) Symmetric groups.** Determine $d_2(\tau_{\rm gen}(S_6))\in\{2,4,6\}$ and $d_1\in6\mathbb Z\cap[6,120]$. Asymptotically, does $d_j(\tau_{\rm gen}(S_n))$ grow linearly in $n$ (as the divisibility bound allows) or super-polynomially (as the root-field bound suggests)? A linear growth would mean that generic étale algebras of degree $n$ become $j$-dimensional after an extension of degree $O(n)$, which would be a strong new upper bound phenomenon; super-polynomial growth would need a new lower-bound technique beyond $\mathrm{ed}(\,\cdot\,;q)$.

**(Q4) Independence of the profile from $V$.** $\mathrm{ed}_k(\tau_{\rm gen})$ does not depend on the faithful $V$; the profile is defined for the specific torsor $\tau_{\rm gen}(V)$ over $k(V)^G$. Is the profile of $\tau_{\rm gen}(V)$ independent of $V$? Both $\tau_{\rm gen}(V)$ and $\tau_{\rm gen}(V')$ are pullbacks of each other after purely transcendental base change ($V\oplus V'$), and (B4) shows $\mathrm{ed}$ is invariant under such base change; but a witness $F'$ over $k(V\oplus V')^G$ need not descend to $k(V)^G$. [open; the answer is presumably yes for $\mathrm{ed}^{[d]}$ by a specialisation argument in the spirit of Reichstein–Scavia, but I have not checked it.]
